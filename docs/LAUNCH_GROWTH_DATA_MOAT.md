# FUN Launch Growth and Data-Moat Plan

## Summary
FUN should launch as a trust-first, one-pick recommendation product. The goal is not broad browse behavior; it is to own the moment when someone wants a single emotionally right answer fast. The launch strategy should build fame through shareable usefulness, then earn through affiliate conversion and later premium personalization.

## First Launch
1. Launch with one strong promise
- One perfect pick for your mood.
- Fast response.
- Honest no-match when the system cannot confidently satisfy the request.
- No endless lists and no fake confidence.

2. Launch audience in phases
- Phase 1: people with decision fatigue who want a better nightly pick.
- Phase 2: language-lane users and subscription-constrained users.
- Phase 3: niche taste users who care about discovery and emotional precision.

3. Launch product priorities
- Make the hero recommendation feel immediate.
- Tighten format, language, and subscription honesty.
- Reduce avoidable no-matches on solvable prompts.
- Make share cards a core virality surface, not a side feature.

## Marketing
1. Positioning
- Stop scrolling. Get one pick.
- Mood recommendation that respects your time, language, and subscriptions.
- Honest no-match when it cannot do better.

2. Content themes
- Decision fatigue stories.
- Before/after examples: long scrolling versus a fast pick.
- Cultural lane examples: Hindi, Korean, French, German.
- Emotional-intelligence examples: comfort, grief, breakup, panic, weird-but-safe.

3. Channels
- Social sharing from the app itself.
- Community posts in movie, TV, and indie communities.
- Short demos showing the recommendation card and why-it-fits reasoning.
- Product-led launch only after the trust bar is high enough.

## WAU
1. Build weekly habit, not one-off novelty
- Add a “Did you watch the pick?” memory loop.
- Use ratings to improve future recommendations.
- Resurface recent picks and taste patterns.

2. Retention loops
- One-tap feedback after watching.
- Save and resurface recent picks.
- Share-card loop for social virality.
- Weekly “one pick for tonight” entry point.

3. Re-engagement
- Light reminders only if users opt in.
- Personalized weekly recaps.
- “Try another mood” prompts after a completed session.

4. Metrics to watch
- Time to first recommendation.
- Recommendation acceptance rate.
- Feedback completion rate.
- Repeat-use within 7 days.
- Share-card creation rate.
- No-match rate on solvable requests.

## Expansion
1. Expand by use case before geography
- Stronger emotional-intent handling.
- Better language lanes.
- Better subscription verification.
- Richer family, transit, partner, and group-watch contexts.

2. Expand by surface
- Share cards.
- Memory and taste profile.
- Subscription-aware recommendations.
- Similar-but-different follow-up picks.

3. Expand by market
- Localized launch packs for India, UK, Poland, and France.
- Keep the core product identity stable, but localize examples and onboarding.

4. Monetization path
- Affiliate links where compliant and transparent.
- Premium subscription for stronger personalization, more memory, and advanced filters.
- Creator or community packs for curated workflows.
- Later B2B or licensing if the recommendation layer becomes strong enough.

## Data Moat
1. Store the right signals
- Prompt text and selected controls.
- Derived intent contract.
- Recommendation returned.
- Availability state.
- Time to first pick.
- User feedback and whether they watched it.
- Repetition and reroll behavior.

2. Learn the right things
- Which moods fail closed too often.
- Which prompts produce wrong-lane picks.
- Which language and subscription combinations are fragile.
- Which titles are accepted, skipped, or rated well by segment.

3. Improve the right systems
- Intent parsing.
- Contract normalization.
- Fallback ranking.
- No-match thresholds.
- Segment-specific recommendation quality.

4. Non-negotiable principle
- The data moat should improve honesty and fit, not just clicks.
- If the system learns to optimize engagement at the cost of trust, it will damage the core product.

## Assumptions
- FUN launches web-first.
- Trust and recommendation quality matter more than breadth at launch.
- The team is willing to trade some recall for higher trust on sensitive and subscription-constrained requests.
