# FUN Launch Geography Plan

## Summary
FUN should launch in stages, starting with markets that are easy to adopt, easy to share, and most likely to produce high-signal feedback for the recommendation engine. The first wave should favor strong English proficiency, high internet usage, mature streaming behavior, and low friction for product understanding. That gives the team the best chance to build WAU, trust, and a usable data moat before expanding into harder localization markets.

## Target Launch Order

### Wave 1: Early conversion and clean feedback
1. United Kingdom and Ireland
- Best first launch market because the product reads naturally in English and the “one pick” value prop is immediately understandable.
- Strong fit for sharing, fast adoption, and clean feedback on mood wording.

2. Netherlands, Nordics, Austria, Germany, Switzerland
- High English proficiency and strong digital maturity make these markets low-friction for preview launch.
- Good for testing language lanes, subscription constraints, and higher-intent premium behavior.
- The Netherlands, Austria, Germany, Norway, Denmark, and Sweden are especially strong fits based on current EF English Proficiency Index rankings.

### Wave 2: Constraint-heavy, high-signal markets
3. Poland and Czech Republic
- Useful for proving the product outside English-native markets without jumping immediately into the hardest localization problem.
- Good for learning from strict runtime, subscription, and no-match behavior.

4. India
- Important for the data moat because it yields high-volume language diversity, Hindi-lane usage, hidden-gem discovery behavior, and more subscription-scarce cases.
- Better after the core recommendation contract is stable enough to avoid trust damage.

### Wave 3: Broad public expansion
5. United States and Canada
- High footfall and strong brand upside, but also more competition and higher expectations.
- Best opened once recommendation quality, speed, and sharing are already strong enough to stand out.

## Why This Order
- Start where adoption friction is lowest.
- Use early markets to harden mood matching, subscription honesty, and response speed.
- Expand into India to grow language and discovery data.
- Open the US and Canada only after the product has enough trust and sharpness to feel differentiated.

## What Each Wave Teaches The Data Moat
- UK and Ireland: baseline behavior, sharing, and first-pick conversion.
- Europe: language discipline, subscription discipline, and premium willingness.
- Poland and Czech Republic: non-US usage patterns and constraint-heavy sessions.
- India: Hindi lane, discovery behavior, and emotionally specific prompts at scale.
- US and Canada: broad consumer behavior and monetization sensitivity.

## Implementation Notes
- Keep launch messaging narrow: one perfect pick, no scrolling, honest no-match.
- Localize examples before fully localizing the UI.
- Use region-specific examples in marketing and onboarding so users see themselves in the product immediately.
- Monitor response time, no-match rate, repeat-title rate, and share-card creation rate by region from day one.

## Assumptions
- FUN remains web-first at launch.
- The main goal is fastest adoption plus the highest-value feedback loop, not maximum total market size on day one.
- The team wants to protect the trust signal of honest no-match behavior rather than force weak recommendations.
